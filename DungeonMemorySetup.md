# Death Results and Dungeon Memory

## Scene Setup

The scripts do not change your existing scene hierarchy or create placeholder art.

1. Add `DungeonMemory` to an active scene manager object in the dungeon scene. Assign **Male Body Sprite** and **Female Body Sprite**. Optionally assign a body prefab with a `SpriteRenderer` and your preferred world sorting settings. Without a prefab, a sprite object is created using the walkable tilemap's sorting layer and order + 1.
2. On the `DungeonMemory` component, use its context menu **Refresh Item Catalog**. Adding the component in the Inspector initially populates this automatically. Refresh again after adding item assets, and save the scene. Catalog references retain the items in builds; saved loot uses asset GUIDs rather than runtime instance IDs or item names.
3. Create a dedicated **Screen Space - Overlay** canvas, separate from the black-fade canvas and outside its `CanvasGroup`. Add `DeathResultsPanel` and assign its **Results Canvas** reference.
4. Under that canvas, create a panel with a `RectTransform`, your panel image, and a `CanvasGroup`. Give it its intended final size, position, and scale in the scene. Assign its `CanvasGroup` to **Panel**. The slam animation changes only local scale and restores it exactly; position and dimensions are left intact.
5. Add five TMP texts under the panel and assign **Character Name Text**, **Kills Text**, **Mini Boss Kills Text**, **Rooms Cleared Text**, and **Floors Entered Text**, in that order. Place the name above the other rows: it displays `Name: <character name>` and slams first after the panel. Text content is set automatically. Layout groups may be used; rows stay in the layout while invisible.
6. Add a sixth TMP text as a **sibling of the panel**, not inside it, and assign **Outcome Text**. Place it where the final death/reanimation message should appear after the panel fades out.
7. On the existing `DeadSceneTransition`, assign **Death Results**, **Dungeon Memory**, and **Floor Manager**. Missing scene references are also discovered automatically. Keep the existing vignette, black-fade group, and main-menu scene assignment. The results canvas is hidden at startup and sorted above the black-fade canvas during the sequence.
8. Enable **Is Mini Boss** and fill **Enemy Name** under `EnemyHealth > Run Statistics` for mini-boss prefabs. Existing `EnemyLich` enemies are classified automatically for kill counts. Names can also be assigned on regular enemies, but their boss name label stays empty while **Is Mini Boss** is false.
9. Add a TMP text to the existing boss health bar and assign **Boss Name Text** on `BossHealthBarUI`. It displays `<Enemy Name> | Lvl <final runtime level>`. Regular bosses use `EnemyLevelXP.Level`, already including the spawner roll. Fallen configuration replaces Enemy Name with `Fallen Descender <remembered name>` and uses `FallenDescenderLevelXP.Level`. Dead/unbound bosses and unnamed enemies clear the label. Keep this text inside the existing fading CanvasGroup.

`BreakablePotManager` must remain active and have its usual loot prefab assigned. Remembered loot uses its exact-item overload and the same A* walkability checks as pot drops. Body and loot objects belong to the selected generated room and are cleaned up with that floor.

## Fallen Descender Prefab

1. Create an enemy prefab with `FallenDescender` on the root. It requires `EnemyHealth`, `Rigidbody2D`, `EnemyElevationLevel`, and `FallenDescenderLevelXP`; Unity adds those automatically. For an existing prefab, remove `EnemyLevelXP` and add `FallenDescenderLevelXP` instead. The spawn point also adds the new tracker if needed and disables a leftover regular XP component. Add a non-trigger `Collider2D` for the body. Use your usual enemy layer, never the Player tag/layer. Gravity is disabled and rotation frozen by the brain. Do not attach `Player`, `PlayerStats`, player input/movement scripts, `EquipmentManager`, or any of the regular enemy FSMs.
2. Add `FallenDescenderRenderer` to the root or a child. Create its paperdoll children and assign the nine SpriteRenderer fields: weapon-under, body, legs, torso, eyes, hair, headwear, weapon-over, and shield. Give the renderers the intended relative sorting orders. Assign the renderer to **FallenDescender > Character Renderer**. A root SortingGroup is recommended.
3. Create dedicated male/female rotting body and eye `CharacterPartDefinition` assets. Assign them to **Rotting Parts**. Populate Idle, Running, Melee and Cast animation frames. Normal armour, weapon, shield and hair definitions use their existing assets. The saved hair is retained; saved body and eye artwork is not reused. A saved hidden helmet stays hidden without hiding the hair.
4. Add a child fire point and assign **FallenDescender > Fire Point**. It should be near the weapon/hand, outside the body's collider. Basic attacks use the remembered weapon's ItemData range, damage types, projectile prefab, speed, homing setting and attack visual. Regular attack cooldown is the weapon's cooldown in seconds multiplied by 1.5; skill cooldown is multiplied by 2.5, without the current player's cooldown reduction. The Fallen automatically adds/configures WeaponController to reuse the weapon's normal skills, including area/slash/stab/spin attacks, arrow rain, Typhoon, Veil of Fire, fireball patterns, charged arrows, beams and crossbow explosions/fire patches. Ready skills take priority within their range; targeted skills aim at the player and charged skills release at full charge. Active charging/sequences cancel on death or leaving the encounter. Melee skills use Melee frames; other skills/ranged/spell attacks use Cast frames, retaining the four animation states. Do not add player input scripts.
5. Tune detection range, repath interval, attack windup, recovery duration and death fade duration on the brain. Movement uses the saved effective sprint speed with no sprint-resource drain. `FallenDescenderLevelXP` tracks the previous-run level plus the optional spawner level modifier without scaling any stats. Set its **Experience Reward** to the level-1 base reward (default 10) and leave **Grant Experience On Death** enabled to grant XP on defeat.
6. On the dungeon's existing `EnemySpawnerManager`, open **Fallen Descender Configuration**, add this prefab to **Enemy Prefabs**, and configure Health, Resistance, Damage, Level and Experience Reward modifier rolls. These have the same min/max/chance controls as regular boss modifiers. Level defaults to zero; its rounded roll is added to the saved level, clamped to at least 1, for the name label and XP reward only. The original saved level remains unchanged and still controls the combat balance gap. There is no floor range or additional appearance roll here: Dungeon Memory owns the encounter chances. One successful reanimation replaces the floor's regular boss selection and regular enemies in its chosen boss room.
7. Refresh **DungeonMemory > Refresh Item Catalog** again. Item Catalog includes all ItemData assets; Hair Catalog includes only CharacterPartDefinition assets whose **Type** is **Hair**. Ensure your hair definitions have that type selected. Save the dungeon scene, then create a new death to test. Existing body records still work; older reanimation records without combat snapshots are retained but skipped because their original stats and gear cannot be reconstructed.

The boss room reports cleared only after the Fallen Descender has faded and completed its corpse/loot handoff. Regular boss tracking and boss UI events continue to use `EnemyHealth`.

Assign the dungeon scene's DungeonMemory, DeathResultsPanel and FloorManager to DeadSceneTransition. The death-memory and floor-manager references can also be found automatically when left empty; do not assign prefab assets instead of the scene components. Seeing the reanimated result does not guarantee an encounter on the next run: the saved death must be valid, the new run must reach its death floor or higher, and the 50%/25% encounter roll must succeed. For a controlled test, temporarily set DungeonMemory's Body Spawn Chance and Later Floor Spawn Chance to 1, then restore 0.5/0.25. Incomplete legacy snapshots (including empty stat/equipment lists) are retained but skipped before rolling, so they cannot block newer valid characters. A rejected Fallen now logs the exact missing prefab/weapon requirement in the Console.

## Behavior

- Sequence: existing red vignette and death delay, fade to black, panel slam, name row slam, four statistics row slams, results hold, panel fade out, outcome fade in/hold/out, then the main menu.
- All presentation timing uses unscaled time. Slam sizes, impact scale, timing, and hold durations are Inspector settings. The existing minimum death delay remains five seconds.
- Kills excludes mini-bosses. Rooms are counted once per generated room's confirmed clear. Floors are counted once per floor number, including the starting floor. Counters freeze on death and reset when a new `Player` is created.
- **Reanimation Chance** defaults to 0.5. Dead records spawn corpses; compatible Reanimated records spawn fightable Fallen Descenders through the boss spawner. Both outcomes preserve the prior character's full carried item units, combat stats, equipment modifiers, hair and helmet-visibility setting.
- At death, a random 25-50% of carried **item units** is selected without replacement from backpack, equipped items, and any dragged item. Duplicate references are not counted twice, and stacks may be partially selected. Integer counts are rounded to the valid range; a one-item inventory drops that one item. Sampling does not alter the dying character's inventory.
- The active starting weapon is included even when it bypasses `EquipmentManager`. A registered weapon is not duplicated by this fallback.
- When the player dies unarmed, the combat snapshot uses the first Weapon in the saved class's Starting Gear on CharacterCustomizationController. The snapshot stores the class and fallback weapon asset ID, so a different class on the next run does not change it. This fallback does not grant an extra loot item: only items actually carried can drop. Create a new death to capture this information; an old empty snapshot cannot reconstruct an unknown class.
- The latest five deaths are persisted across game restarts using the `Sitanite.DungeonMemory.v1` PlayerPrefs key. A sixth saved death discards the oldest, whether it is dead or reanimated.
- Both outcomes get a 50% appearance roll on the recorded death floor and a 25% roll on each higher floor if they have not appeared. Rolls happen once per memory per floor per character, preventing regeneration/reload rerolls. No encounter occurs below the recorded floor. Failed rolls stay saved for later floors and future runs. A successfully spawned Fallen Descender cannot repeat on another floor in that run; its record remains available in future runs if it survives.
- Successful bodies are placed on a random registered walkable tile in a generated room on that floor. Saved loot is scattered nearby on the same walkable tilemap. Placement waits one frame for generated A* maps to register.
- A record is removed only after its body and all sampled loot have been created successfully. Missing art, unresolved item assets, or failed loot placement retain the record. Invalid archive data is reported without overwriting the stored save.
- When a Fallen Descender is defeated, it fades out, leaves the matching corpse, and drops a random 50-75% of its original full carried item units, including equipped gear. This is not 50-75% of the already reduced corpse loot. Weapons and armour share the random sample; any specific equipped item can be omitted. Equipment found in the combat snapshot but missing from an older full-loot list is included in the pool without duplicating already captured gear; fallback combat-only weapons are excluded. Assigned body prefabs are made visible and sorted above the walkable floor. A death outside a registered tile falls back to a walkable tile in its room. If corpse placement fails, the saved encounter becomes a dead-body record carrying that reward sample for a future attempt.
- Hostile projectile instances use the Player hittable-layer mask. Spell/Arrow/ChargedArrow, BaseArrow, beams, Typhoon, Veil of Fire and crossbow explosion/fire effects route damage to PlayerStats only with elevation checks and multi-collider deduplication. Shared ItemData and projectile prefab layer settings are not modified, so normal player weapons keep their original behavior.

## Boss Balance

The signed level gap is `currentPlayerLevel - previousEnemyLevel`. It is negative when the old character is higher-level, as requested.

```text
base damage     = max(0, previous pre-equipment damage + level gap)
base resistance = max(0, previous pre-equipment resistance + level gap)
health          = previous pre-equipment max health + current player max health / 2
```

Then apply half of the saved gear's flat and percentage modifiers, using the equipment formula `(value + half flat bonuses) * (1 + half percentage bonuses / 100)`. Weapon damage entries are included at half strength. Resistance applies the BaseDamageResistance stage followed by DamageResistance, matching PlayerStats. Finally add the Fallen Descender Configuration rolls. Health is clamped to at least 1; damage and resistance to at least 0. Only types actually declared in the saved weapon's damage entries are emitted by attacks; unrelated base damage values are never applied.

Existing mini-bosses use their normal level-scaled stats as the prior enemy values, add half the current player's maximum health, and apply the same signed level-gap adjustment to damage/resistance. Their ordinary spawner modifiers are added afterward. They have no player armour/weapon snapshot to halve. The configured health/resistances are protected against later automatic enemy level scaling.

Fallen Descender level is its saved previous-run level plus the optional rounded spawner Level roll, clamped to at least 1. A zero roll preserves the saved level. Its dedicated XP component never scales health, damage, resistance or movement. Its defeat reward is `base EXP reward + spawner EXP bonus + 20 * max(0, final level - 1)`, with the base plus bonus clamped to at least zero. With the default base of 10 and no spawner bonus, final levels 1/2/3/5 grant 10/30/50/90 EXP. Regular enemies keep their existing `EnemyLevelXP` behavior. Instant skill damage uses the balanced primary damage plus the weapon's raw skill damage, with the existing secondary/tertiary slot multipliers; charged skills use raw charge damage plus half balanced primary damage. No ItemData assets or the current player's equipped gear are modified.

## Verification

In Edit Mode, run **Tools > Dungeon Memory > Run Checks**. The checks use temporary objects and restore the A* singleton and random state; they do not modify saved deaths or authored scene objects. Results are logged to the Console and written to `Temp/DungeonMemoryChecks.result`.

For just the new name presentation, run **Tools > Dungeon Memory > Run Name Checks**. This checks the first-row name reveal, unchanged statistics/outcome text, mini-boss names and final levels, Fallen name replacement and level modifiers without combat scaling, and blank regular/dead/unbound labels. It logs its own result to the Console independently of the full suite.

Checks cover five-record eviction, combat/full-loot JSON round-tripping, 128 seeded corpse and Fallen loot samples, equipment deduplication, starting-weapon inclusion, separate mini-boss counters, duplicate notifications, frozen statistics, new-character reset, per-floor rolls, encounter suppression, boss formulas, weapon-type filtering, scaling protection, Fallen saved-level XP and unchanged stats, legacy XP suppression, single defeat rewards, delayed Fallen room clears, authored slam restoration and sparse walkable-map placement.

After assigning the scene UI and sprites, verify in Play Mode:

1. Defeat regular enemies and a mini-boss, clear a room, and enter another floor. Die and confirm `Name: <dying character name>` appears first after the panel, then the four values, followed by the outcome and menu transition. Confirm the slam ends at the authored panel/text transforms. Confirm boss labels use the assigned names and final levels, and regular enemies leave the name label blank.
2. Temporarily set **Reanimation Chance** to 0 and **Body Spawn Chance** to 1. Die with stacked inventory items and equipped gear, create a new character, and reach the same floor. Confirm the matching gender sprite, pickup-enabled loot next to it, and no drops outside the walkable map.
3. Set **Body Spawn Chance** to 0 and repeat across runs. Restore it to 1 and confirm earlier deaths can still appear. Creating six cached deaths should evict the oldest.
4. Set **Reanimation Chance** to 1 and **Body Spawn Chance** to 1. Create a fresh death with armour and a weapon, then start a new run. Confirm a Fallen Descender replaces the regular boss, wears the saved gear/hair, uses the saved weapon's basic attack and drops its corpse/50-75% loot after fading. Compare its health/damage/resistance with the formulas above and confirm the boss bar and room-clear gateway work.
5. Test a melee weapon, a ranged weapon and a spell weapon, including a weapon with multiple damage slots. Confirm regular/skill cooldowns use the 1.5/2.5 multipliers, skills match the held weapon and all projectiles/area effects damage only the player. Check a charged weapon and interrupt it by killing the Fallen. Die unarmed as each class, then confirm the reanimation uses that class's Starting Gear weapon without adding an extra drop. Test stair pursuit and a different room to confirm the boss stays in its encounter room.
6. Set **Body Spawn Chance** to 0 and **Later Floor Spawn Chance** to 1. Confirm a missed death-floor corpse/reanimation can appear on a higher floor, but cannot be rerolled by regenerating the same floor. Restore Body Spawn Chance to 0.5, Later Floor Spawn Chance to 0.25 and Reanimation Chance to 0.5 after testing.
